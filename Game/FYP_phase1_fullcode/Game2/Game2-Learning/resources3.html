States Of Matter

Solids

Solids have a definite shape and volume. The molecules are closely packed together, and they don't move much. Examples of solids include ice, wood, and metals.

Liquids

Liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container. The molecules are loosely packed and can move freely. Water, oil, and milk are examples of liquids.

Gases

Gases have neither a definite shape nor a definite volume. The molecules are far apart and move freely. Examples include oxygen, carbon dioxide, and helium.

Changes in Matter

Reversible Changes

Changes which can be reversed are called reversible changes. If you keep water in the freezer for some time, it is transformed into ice. If it is taken out, it becomes water again. This is a reversible change.

Irreversible Changes

Changes which cannot be reversed are called irreversible changes. If you burn a piece of paper it burns and turns into ash. It cannot become paper again.
